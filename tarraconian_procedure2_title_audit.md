## 1. Executive Summary & Audit Metrics

To prevent armchair etymology and enforce realistic medieval scribal conventions, the **Imperial Chancellery** executes **Procedure 2 (Sovereign Cultural Lineage & Etymological Transparency Protocol)** across all candidate descriptive titles.

Toponyms are classified into three distinct **Toponym Adoption Patterns** depending on whether the etymological meaning remained living, transitional, or suffered complete semantic rupture:

| Toponym Adoption Pattern | Continuity Tier | Qualifying Condition in CK3 Sovereign History | Chancellery Outcome | Title Count |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :---: |
| **Pattern 1: Preferred Semantic Calque** | **Tier 1: High Living Continuity** | Annexed directly from naming culture ruler OR unbroken native population | Full semantic translation adopted as primary name (*Stûlwîtzenburch*, *Wîtzenburch*) | **74** |
| **Pattern 2: Hybrid Calque Allowed** | **Tier 2: Transitional Dynastic Rule** | Foreign rule lasted $< 50$ years AND local population intact | Dual designation: primary phonetic or hybrid with approved secondary calque (*Temeschburch*, *Calatajût*) | **8** |
| **Pattern 3: Strict Phonetic Nativization** | **Tier 3: Complete Semantic Rupture** | Alien rule persisted $\ge 50–100$ years OR population converted | Immutable phonetic token; semantic calque rejected as primary (*Tschongrât*, *Sevîlje*) | **30** |
| **Rule 3: Calquing Prohibited** | **Control: Opaque Eponyms** | Personal names, tribal founders, or pre-Indo-European stems | Calquing strictly forbidden; pure phonetic adaptation (*Batsch*, *Bodroch*, *Parîs*) | **5** |
| **Total Evaluated Titles** | — | — | — | **125** |
