# Tarraconian Linguistic & Sound-Change Guide (ca. 1142 Update)

**Culture:** Tarraconian (*Tarracono-Lothringian*, `ID 316` — 744 counties in 1142, up from 547 in 1122)  
**Daughter Dialects & Languages:**  
* **Alpine-Tarraconian** (*Alpîn-Tarraconisch*, `ID 337` — hybridized 1042 with Cisalpine)  
* **Croato-Tarraconian** (*Kroatisch-Tarraconisch*, `ID 345` — hybridized 1057 with Croatian, 14 counties in 1142)  
* **Ungwârer** (*Ungwârerisch / Ungwâri*, `ID 367` — diverged 1094 in Upper Hungary, Magyar-speaking daughter language with Central Germanic heritage & Franconian onomasticon, 2 counties in 1142)  

**Linguistic Classification:** High German (Moselle/Rhine Franconian superstrate) with a heavy Old Catalan / Occitano-Romance substrate and Hispano-Latin administrative lexicon.

---

## 1. Sociolinguistic Overview: A Romano-Germanic Imperial Tongue

Formed on **31 August 989** from the hybridization of **Lothringian** (a bureaucratic Franconian culture speaking High German) and **Catalan** (a courtly, maritime Occitano-Romance culture of the Spanish March / *Hispania Tarraconensis*), **Tarraconian** represents a historical "reverse Frankish" scenario.

Rather than abandoning their Germanic tongue for Romance, the Lothringian imperial elite of House *Chatenois-Metz* retained **11th/12th-century High German** (`language_high_german`) and **Franconian personal names** (`name_list_franconian`), while absorbing **Catalan courtly culture** (`ethos_courtly`), **Mediterranean maritime mercantilism**, **Iberian architecture**, and an **Imperial Magistracy** system of governance. By **1142**, following the restoration of **The Roman Empire** (`h_roman_empire` / **Dat Rômische Keiserrîch**) under Kaiser Adémar "the Great" and Augustus Wernhard "the Bear", Tarraconian serves as the universal chancellery language across **100 Kingdoms** (**451 Duchies** and **1,703 De Jure Counties**, of which **1,513** are inside the Roman Empire) from the Hebrides and Sápmi to Khorasan, Sindh, and the Horn of Africa.

The result is a language structurally parallel to Middle English (Germanic grammar + Anglo-Norman lexicon), but with roles and geographies reversed:
* **Core Grammar, Morphology & Phonology:** Early Middle High German (Moselle/Rhine Franconian dialect of Metz, Trier, and Mainz).
* **Administrative, Courtly & Nautical Lexicon:** Old Catalan, Occitan, and Medieval Hispano-Latin, supplemented after the Imperial Restoration by Byzantine Greek administrative borrowings.
* **Chancellery Orthography:** High German phonemes written through the scribal conventions of Catalan-Aragonese and Imperial chancelleries.

## 2. Orthography & IPA Pronunciation Index

Tarraconian scribes write their Moselle-Franconian High German using a blend of **Middle High German** and **Old Catalan** spelling conventions. Circumflexes (`â, ê, î, ô, û` / `Â, Ê, Î, Ô, Û`) indicate long vowels. Capitalization does not alter vowel quantity: capital letters systematically retain their circumflexes (e.g., **Ôsterrîch**, **Îslant**, **Âchen**, **Ûtrecht**, **Êvore**), without omission or substitution.

## 3. Core Phonological & Morphological Rules

### Rule 1: The Rhine-Moselle Franconian Consonant Profile
Because Tarraconian descends from **Lothringian** (`name_list_franconian`), it follows the **West Central German (Moselle/Rhine Franconian)** branch of the High German Consonant Shift:
1. **Shifted *t* $\rightarrow$ *z/ss* and *k* $\rightarrow$ *ch*:** Post-vocalic *-k* becomes `-ch` (*Rîch*), and initial/post-consonantal *t-* becomes `z-` / `tz` (*Zips*, *Mênz*). This shift applies systematically to foreign post-vocalic velar stops (*Baalbek* $\rightarrow$ **Bâlbech**, *Mělník* $\rightarrow$ **Melnîch**, *Ak* $\rightarrow$ **Ach**, *Arkalyk* $\rightarrow$ **Arkalîch**, *Terek* $\rightarrow$ **Terech**).
2. **Unshifted *p*, *d*, and Initial *sw- / sl- / sm-*:** Unlike Bavarian or Swabian, Tarraconian keeps initial `p-` unshifted (**Palz** "Palace/Pfalz", **Port** "Harbor"), retains `d-` where Upper German has *t-* (**Dat** "The", **Dorf** "Village", **Nider-** "Lower"), and preserves Middle High German `sw-`, `sl-`, `sm-` (**Swâben**, **Slêswîch**, **Smalkalden**, **Swînvurt**) without New High German *Schw-/Schl-/Schm-* spellings.
3. **Final Devoicing (*Auslautverhärtung*) + Catalan `-ch`:** Both Middle High German and Old Catalan systematically devoice word-final obstruents across all etymological strata:
   * Word-final `-g` and `-k` merge into **`-ch`** (`[x ~ ç]`): *Burg* $\rightarrow$ **Burch** `[bʊrx]`, *Berg* $\rightarrow$ **Berch** `[bɛrç]`, *Mark* + Catalan *Marca* $\rightarrow$ **March** `[marx]`, *Baalbek* $\rightarrow$ **Bâlbech**, *Mělník* $\rightarrow$ **Melnîch**.
   * Word-final `-d` and `-b` devoice to **`-t`** and **`-p`**: *Land* $\rightarrow$ **Lant** (**Dütschlant**, **Engellant**, **Vrîslant**), *Ohrid* $\rightarrow$ **Ochrît**, *Baghdad* $\rightarrow$ **Bagdât**, *Gwened* $\rightarrow$ **Gwenet**, *Ḥalab* $\rightarrow$ **Halep**, *Gharb* $\rightarrow$ **Gârp**.
   * Word-final affricates devoice to **`-tsch`** (`[tʃ]`): word-final voiced `*-dsch` is **strictly illicit** and must devoice to voiceless **`-tsch`** (*Najd* $\rightarrow$ **Netsch**, *Aflaj* $\rightarrow$ **Falâtsch**).
   * Foreign word-final `-ov / -ev / -av` vocalizes to the MHG diphthongal suffix **`-ouwe`** (`[ɔʊ.wə]`): *Opava* $\rightarrow$ **Troppouwe**, *Moskva* $\rightarrow$ **Moskouwe**, *Rostov* $\rightarrow$ **Rostouwe**, *Azov* $\rightarrow$ **Asouwe**, *Preslav* $\rightarrow$ **Preslouwe**.

### Rule 2: Dual-Substrate Apocope & Final Vowel Reduction
Old Catalan drops unstressed final vowels (*-o, -e*) after most consonants, while Middle High German reduces unstressed final vowels to schwa `-e` `[ə]`. When Romance, Greek, Latin, Slavic, or Arabic place names enter Tarraconian High German:
* Final `-o`, `-um`, `-us`, `-os`, `-on`, and `-polis` are apocopated or reduced:
  * *Tarraconem / Tarragona* $\rightarrow$ **Tarracôn** `[ta.ra.ˈkoːn]`
  * *Lanciano* $\rightarrow$ **Lantschân** `[lan.ˈtʃaːn]`, *Butrinto* $\rightarrow$ **Butrînt** `[bu.ˈtrɪnt]`
  * *Alençon* $\rightarrow$ **Alenzûn** `[a.lɛn.ˈtsuːn]`
  * *Ephesos* $\rightarrow$ **Ephês** `[ɛ.ˈfeːs]`, *Korinthos* $\rightarrow$ **Korînt** `[ko.ˈrɪnt]`, *Dorostorum* $\rightarrow$ **Durostôr**, *Damaskos* $\rightarrow$ **Damasch**
  * *Kallipolis* $\rightarrow$ **Kallîpel**, *Nikopolis* $\rightarrow$ **Nikôpel**
* Greek `-ion` becomes **`-jen`** (**Ikônjen**, **Amôrjen**, **Filomêljen**), while `-eon`, `-ea`, and `-eia` become **`-êe`** `[eː.ə]` (**Kaisarêe**, **Nîkêe**, **Sebastêe**, **Laodikêe**, **Dorîlêe**).
* Final `-a` / `-ia` and Slavic/Norse `-o / -a` either become `-je` / `-jen` (`[jə ~ jən]`) or `-îe` (`[iː.ə]`), or reduce to High German schwa **`-e`** `[ə]` (**Prâge**, **Lûke**, **Oske**, **Pêtre**, **Dubne**, **Trûse**, **Sigtûne**, **Samosâte**, **Edesse**), or drop after single liquids/nasals (**Tudela** $\rightarrow$ **Tudêl** `[tu.ˈdeːl]`).
* **Exonymic Nativization & Centralization of Final `-a`:** Following authentic medieval European chancellery practice (cf. *Bern* for *Verona*, *Mailand* for *Mediolanum*, *Breslau* for *Wrocław*), non-European simplex stems ending in unaccented `-a` systematically reduce to High German schwa **`-e`** `[ə]` (**Awe**, **Ude**, **Selenge**, **Tunke**, **Akare-Dasarne**, **Saraswate-Mandale**). Non-European geographic generics are calqued directly into native High German topographic suffixes (**Selenga-Tal**, **Uda-Tal**, **Dôndal**, **Duortnos-Dal**), while medieval trade exonyms are adopted in place of modern transcriptions (*Caracaron* / **Karakorôn** per Rubruck and Polo).

### Rule 3: Palatal Assimilation & Slavic `-sk` Adaptation
Because Tarraconian scribes are fluent in both Romance and Germanic sounds, foreign palatals, sibilants, and Slavic suffixes are systematically mapped:
* Romance `ny / nh / gn` (`/ɲ/`) $\rightarrow$ **`nj`** `[ɲ]`: *Armagnac* $\rightarrow$ **Armanjach** `[ar.ma.ˈɲax]`, *Lusignan* $\rightarrow$ **Lusinjan** `[lu.zɪ.ˈɲan]`.
* French/Occitan/Arabic/Turkic `j / g(e,i) / ch / sh / ş` $\rightarrow$ **`sch`** `[ʃ]` or **`dsch`** `[dʒ]`: *Anjou* $\rightarrow$ **Anschou** `[ˈan.ʃɔʊ]`, *Shayzar* $\rightarrow$ **Schêisar** `[ˈʃɛɪ.zar]`, *Jimi* $\rightarrow$ **Dschîmi** `[ˈdʒiː.mi]`.
* **Foreign Affricates (`[dʒ]`, `[dʐ]`, `[tɕ]`) $\rightarrow$ `dsch` `[dʒ]` (or `tsch` `[tʃ]`):** Foreign sounds that modern Latin alphabets transcribe as `j` (Indic `ज` *j*, Korean `ㅈ` *j / -ju*, Pinyin `j`, Japanese *j*, Arabic `ج` *jīm*, Persian `ج`, African *j / dj*, and Kazakh `Ж` *zh/j*) must strictly be rendered as **`dsch`** (or **`tsch`** in voiceless/final environments per Auslautverhärtung), **NEVER** with bare `j`. Bare `j` in Tarraconian is reserved exclusively for the palatal glide `[j]` (German *Jûlich*, *Jûtlant*; Slavic *Jaroslouwe*; Arabic *Janbû*; Pinyin *Jandschou* for *Yanzhou*):
  * Indic: *Jaunpur* $\rightarrow$ **Dschounpur**, *Kanyakubja* $\rightarrow$ **Kanjakubdsche**, *Jejakabhukti* $\rightarrow$ **Dschedschâkabhukti**, *Ajmer* $\rightarrow$ **Adschmêr**, *Ujjayini* $\rightarrow$ **Udschajinî**.
  * Korean: Administrative *-ju* (州) $\rightarrow$ **`-dschu`** (*Gyeongju* $\rightarrow$ **Gjongdschu**, *Gwangju* $\rightarrow$ **Gwangdschu**, *Haeju* $\rightarrow$ **Haedschu**, *Tschungju* $\rightarrow$ **Tschungdschu**); initial *Jang-* $\rightarrow$ **`Dschang-`** (*Jangjeong* $\rightarrow$ **Dschangdschong**).
  * Chinese Pinyin: *Jinzhou* $\rightarrow$ **Dschindschou** (distinct from *Yinzhou* $\rightarrow$ **Jindschou**), *Jingzhou* $\rightarrow$ **Dschingdschou**, *Jiankang* $\rightarrow$ **Dschiankâng**, *Jiezhou* $\rightarrow$ **Dschiedschou**, *Dongji* $\rightarrow$ **Dongdschi**, *Cangjing* $\rightarrow$ **Zangdsching**.
  * Japanese: *Awaji* $\rightarrow$ **Awadschi**, *Tajima* $\rightarrow$ **Tadschime**.
  * African: *Kilimanjaro* $\rightarrow$ **Kilimandscharô**, *Jenne* $\rightarrow$ **Dschenne**, *Futa Jalon* $\rightarrow$ **Futa-Dschalon**, *Dakajalan* $\rightarrow$ **Dakadschalan**.
  * Arabic / Persian / Turkic: *Burjanna* $\rightarrow$ **Burdschâne**, *Jend* $\rightarrow$ **Dschent**, *Jame* $\rightarrow$ **Dschâm**, *Rukhaj* $\rightarrow$ **Ruchatsch**, *Jilanjik* $\rightarrow$ **Dschilantschîch**, *Jitikul* $\rightarrow$ **Dschetiköl**, *Julym* $\rightarrow$ **Tschulîm**, *Ghulja* $\rightarrow$ **Guldsche**.

* **Slavic `-sk` / `-zk` Codas $\rightarrow$ `-tzich` `[tsɪç]` or `-bsch` `[pʃ]`:** Because both High German and Old Catalan palatalized historic `*sk` to `sch` `[ʃ]`, bare word-final `-sk` is phonotactically alien to Tarraconian. Following medieval German chancellery practice (cf. *Gdańsk* $\rightarrow$ *Dantzig/Dantzich*, *Połock* $\rightarrow$ *Plotzke*, *Pinsk* $\rightarrow$ *Pintzke*), Slavic `-sk` is adapted by resolving the sibilant + velar stop into the affricate + Franconian suffix **`-tzich`** `[tsɪç]`: *Słupsk* $\rightarrow$ **Slûptzich**, *Płock* $\rightarrow$ **Plôtzich**, *Połock* $\rightarrow$ **Polôtzich**, *Pińsk* $\rightarrow$ **Pîntzich**, *Kletsk* $\rightarrow$ **Klêtzich**, *Smoleńsk* $\rightarrow$ **Smolêntzich**, *Kursk* $\rightarrow$ **Kûrtzich** (where not resolved to the underlying hydronymic substrate under the Toponym-First Principle, e.g. *Měnsk* $\rightarrow$ **Alt-Mêne** from the Menka River). After labials (`-bsk`), the cluster assimilates directly to **`-bsch`** (*Vitebsk* $\rightarrow$ **Wîtebsch**), or undergoes suffix replacement with **`-ouwe`** (*Petrovsk* $\rightarrow$ **Petrouwe**).

### Rule 4: Resolution of Illicit Consonant Clusters (Epenthesis, Prosthesis & Sonorants)
Foreign onset and coda clusters that violate Middle High German and Occitano-Romance sonority sequencing are repaired by systematic chancellery rules:
* **Schwa/Vowel Epenthesis (Anaptyxis) & Prosthesis:** Unpronounceable initial clusters (`Wsch-`, `Mtzch-`, `Zch-`, `Tm-`, `Ps-`, `Ms-`) and syllabic sonorants (`Trn-`, `Lt-`) insert a short vowel (`e`, `i`, `a`) or prosthetic `A-`: *Wschowa* $\rightarrow$ **Wischouwe**, *Trnovo* $\rightarrow$ **Ternouwe**, *Ltava* $\rightarrow$ **Altâwe**, *Mtskheta* $\rightarrow$ **Metzchête**, *Tmutarakan* $\rightarrow$ **Tamatarchân**, *Pset* $\rightarrow$ **Pesêt**, *Mzab* $\rightarrow$ **Mesâp**.
* **Sibilant Cluster Repair:** Initial `sr-` inserts a homorganic dental stop **`str-`** (*Sredets* $\rightarrow$ **Strêdetz**); redundant `stsch-` simplifies to **`sch-`** (*Szczecin* $\rightarrow$ **Schettîn**, *Szczytno* $\rightarrow$ **Schîtne**); and post-labial palatal glides vocalize to **`-ij-`** (*Zvyahel* $\rightarrow$ **Swijâhel**, *Vyangi* $\rightarrow$ **Wijângen**).
* **Word-Final Syllabic Liquids (`-wl`, `-mr`):** Resolved via morphological adaptation to **`-ouwe`** (*Yaroslavl* $\rightarrow$ **Jaroslouwe**) or schwa epenthesis **`-er`** (*Qimr* $\rightarrow$ **Kîmer**).

### Rule 5: Consonant Cluster Phonotactics & Positional Constraints (Onset, Medial, Coda)
Tarraconian balances Middle High German cluster tolerance with Occitano-Romance sonority restrictions across the syllable template:

1. **Word-Initial Onsets ($\#C_0^3$):**
   * **Stop + Liquid:** Clusters with steep rising sonority are universally permitted: `br-`, `tr-`, `gr-`, `kr-`, `pr-`, `dr-`, `bl-`, `kl-`, `pl-`, `gl-` (**Bragantze**, **Trabûnje**, **Plintenburch**, **Granâde**).
   * **Unshifted Franconian Sibilant + Sonorant:** Preserves early Middle High German `sw-`, `sl-`, `sm-`, `sn-` without New High German *schw-/schl-/schm-* (**Swâben**, **Swanêtjen**, **Slatibôr**, **Smalkalden**, **Snotingham**).
   * **The Affricates (`z-`, `tsch-`, `dsch-` vs. illicit `*ts-`):**
     * **`z-` `[ts]` is the mandatory spelling for initial alveolar affricates:** In word-initial position, `[ts]` is invariably rendered as **`z-`** (**Zips**, **Zang**, **Zugaru**, **Zuschime**, **Zukuschi**, **Zezen**, **Zomai**, **Zidschou**). Modern transliteration `*ts-` (Hepburn, Pinyin, Slavic *ts*) is strictly illicit in chancellery orthography.
     * **`tsch-` `[tʃ]` is 100% sound** as a simple onset across all word positions, adopted through medieval contact with Slavic (*č*), Hungarian (*cs*), Italian (*c-* before *e/i*), and Catalan expressive vocabulary (**Tschoripi**, **Tschugutschach**, **Tschaganijân**, **Batsch**).
     * **`dsch-` `[dʒ]` is phonologically sound initially and medially**, inherited from the Old Catalan substrate (where Latin *j-* and *g(e,i)-* yielded `[dʒ]`, e.g., *joc*, *jove*, *gent*) and vital for transcribing Mediterranean, Arabic (`ج`), and Levantine toponyms (**Dschabal**, **Dschafâre**).
     * *Orthographic note on `dsch`:* While `dsch-` serves as the unambiguous phonemic chancellery spelling in official gazetteers, classical Catalan scribal habit alternatively renders this affricate as **`j-`** (before *a, o, u*) or **`g-`** (before *e, i*) (**Jabal**, **Jafâre**, **Gênua**).
   * **Forbidden Initial Clusters & Repairs:**
     * **Initial Velar Nasal (`*#ŋ`):** Words beginning in `ng-` (*Ngamring*, *Ngirô*) violate European phonotactics; they undergo prosthetic vowel repair (*Ingamring*) or onset simplification (*Gamring*).
     * **Indic Aspirated Stops:** Onsets in `bh-`, `dh-`, `gh-`, `th-`, `sth-` (*Bhaktapur*, *Dhamalpur*, *Sthanisware*) simplify to plain stops `b-`, `d-`, `g-`, `t-`, `st-` (**Baktapur**, **Damalpur**, **Stanisware**).
     * **Foreign `kh-` Spelling:** Scribal `kh-` (*Khitan*, *Khotan*, *Khakass*) is regularized to chancellery **`ch-`** (**Chitan**, **Chotan**, **Chakass**) per Rule 2.2.
     * **Sonority Inversions:** Clusters with falling or flat sonority (`#mr-`, `#mt-`) require vowel epenthesis or prefixing (*Mto Galane* $\rightarrow$ *Gubernat van Galane*).

2. **Word-Final Codas ($C_0^4\#$) & Universal Auslautverhärtung:**
   * **Absolute Devoicing:** Final obstruents are strictly voiceless across all etymological strata:
     * `-b` $\rightarrow$ **`-p`** (*Halep*, *Aidâp*, *Gârp*)
     * `-d` $\rightarrow$ **`-t`** (*Dütschlant*, *Engellant*, *Ochrît*, *Bagdât*, *Gwenet*)
     * `-g` and post-vocalic *-k* $\rightarrow$ **`-ch`** (*Burch*, *Berch*, *March*, *Ach*, *Akkitîch*, *Arkalîch*, *Terech*)
     * `-dsch` $\rightarrow$ **`-tsch`**: Word-final voiced affricate `*-dsch` is **strictly illicit**; it devoices to **`-tsch`** `[tʃ]` (*Najd* $\rightarrow$ **Netsch**, *Aflaj* $\rightarrow$ **Falâtsch**).
   * **Permissible Coda Clusters:** Sonorant + Stop/Fricative/Affricate codas are standard: `-nt` (*Engellant*), `-rch` (*Burch*), `-rt` (*Bedevort*), `-rn` (*Beiern*), `-lt` (*Anhalt*), `-ntz` (*Bisantz*), `-tz` (*Lîgnitz*, *Achtîtz*), `-st` (*Brest*), `-cht` (*Mâstricht*), `-tzich` (*Plôtzich*), `-bsch` (*Wîtebsch*). Foreign final `*-ts` is written **`-tz`**.

3. **Word-Medial Clusters & Syllable Contact:**
   * **Medial Affricate Orthography (`-tz-` vs. illicit `*-ts-`):** Medial `[ts]` is written **`-tz-`** (**Ützang**, **Satzume**, **Gyatze**, **Metzepôle**, **Pêtzame**, **Fantzaô**, **Valentzîe**). The spelling sequence `-ts-` never represents an affricate in native stems and is only found across Germanic compound morpheme boundaries where a morpheme ending in `-t` meets one beginning in `s-` (**Ôstsachsen**, **Sûtsachsen**, **Westsachsen**, **Altsôl**, **Altsandetz**).
   * High-tolerance medial clusters across syllable boundaries are legitimate when sonority falls across the syllable break (Syllable Contact Law):
     * Nasal + Affricate contact: **`-ngdsch-`** `[ŋ.dʒ]` (*Dengdschou*), **`-ndsch-`** `[n.dʒ]` (*Bindschou*), **`-ngtsch-`** `[ŋ.tʃ]` (*Dongtscheziguô*), **`-ntsch-`** `[n.tʃ]` (*Lantschân*).
     * Compounding contact: **`-stvr-`** (*Ôstvrancrîch*, *Westvrîslant*), **`-rnskr-`** (*Arnskrône*), **`-rchsl-`** (*Berchslâgen*).

### Rule 6: Word Stress & Metrical Phonotactics
In Tarraconian, phonotactics and metrical prosody are intrinsically linked:

1. **The Stress-to-Weight Condition (Quantity Sensitivity):**
   Stressed syllables in Tarraconian must be **bimoraic** ($\mu\mu$):
   $$\sigma_{\text{stressed}} \ge \mu\mu \implies [CVː] \quad\text{or}\quad [CVC]$$
   * Stressed open syllables **must** contain a long vowel or diphthong (**Prâge** `[ˈpraː.ɣə]`, **Spîre** `[ˈʃpiː.rə]`, **Keiser** `[ˈkɛɪ.zər]`). A stressed short open vowel ($*C\acute{V}$) is illicit.
   * Stressed short vowels must be closed by a coda consonant (**March** `[ˈmarx]`, **Kent** `[ˈkɛnt]`, **Batsch** `[ˈbatʃ]`).

2. **The Weight-to-Stress Principle (Stress Magnetism):**
   Heavy syllables containing long vowels (**`â, ê, î, ô, û`**) or heavy diphthongs (`ou`, `ei`, `ai`) attract primary or secondary stress:
   * **Tarracôn** `[ta.ra.ˈkoːn]` (stress pulled to final long `ô`)
   * **Adrianôpel** `[a.dri.a.ˈnoː.pəl]` (stress pulled to antepenultimate long `ô`)
   * **Lantschân** `[lan.ˈtʃaːn]`, **Tudêl** `[tu.ˈdeːl]`, **Abbadân** `[a.ba.ˈdaːn]`

3. **Dual Metrical Cadence (Germanic Trochaic vs. Catalan Oxytone/Paroxytone):**
   Tarraconian balances two metrical rhythmic systems depending on etymological provenance:
   * **Germanic Lexical Stock (Trochaic, Left-Prominent):** Primary stress falls on the root syllable with a strong-weak (`ˈσ σ`) foot cadence:
     * **Kûnincrich** `[ˈkyː.nɪŋk.riːç]`, **Dütschlant** `[ˈdyːtʃ.lant]`, **Everwîch** `[ˈɛ.vər.wiːç]`, **Bedevort** `[ˈbɛ.də.vɔrt]`.
   * **Romance / Catalan Substrate Stock (Right-Prominent):**
     * **Oxytone (Final Stress):** Words ending in a consonant following historical apocope carry stress on the final syllable: **Castel** `[kas.ˈtɛl]`, **Tolêt** `[to.ˈleːt]`, **Ruwân** `[ru.ˈwaːn]`, **Bastîe** `[bas.ˈtiː.ə]`.
     * **Paroxytone (Penultimate Stress):** Words ending in unstressed reduced schwa **`-e`** `[ə]` retain penultimate stress: **Sevîlje** `[sə.ˈviː.ʎə]`, **Mêride** `[ˈmeː.ri.də]`, **Oske** `[ˈɔs.kə]`, **Wande** `[ˈwan.də]`.

4. **Stress-Governed Asymmetry:**
   * **Complexity under Stress:** Heavy consonant clusters ($C_0^3 V C_0^4$) are permissible only when anchored to a stressed root syllable (*Strâtzburch* `[ˈʃtraːts.bʊrx]`, *Fürstbisdom* `[ˈfʏrst.bɪs.doːm]`).
   * **Reduction in Unstressed Syllables:** Unstressed syllables cannot support heavy vowel contrasts or complex codas. Vowels reduce to schwa `[ə]` or apocopate, and complex foreign codas trigger vowel epenthesis (*Qimr* $\rightarrow$ **Kîmer**) or trochaic suffix remodeling (*Yaroslavl* $\rightarrow$ **Jaroslouwe**).

## 6. Daughter Dialects & Daughter Languages of Tarraconian (ca. 1122)

As the Roman Empire expanded across diverse linguistic zones between 989 and 1122, three distinct daughter varieties crystallized from Tarraconian:

### 6.1 Alpine-Tarraconian (*Alpîn-Tarraconisch*, `ID 337` — Formed 1042)
* **Linguistic Classification:** Italo-Franconian High German dialect (`language_high_german`) with a heavy **Cisalpine (Gallo-Italic)** and Lombardic adstrate.
* **Spoken Zone (1122):** Piedmont, Lombardy, Emilia, and Tuscia (`c_tortona`, `c_aosta`, `c_canavese`, `c_bologna`, `c_arezzo`, plus colonial `c_volodymyr`).
* **Distinctive Phonological & Lexical Features:**
  1. **Gallo-Italic Vocalic Fronting:** Influenced by Cisalpine/Piedmontese, Alpine-Tarraconian speakers front long `û` `[uː]` toward `[yː]` (`Lûke` $\rightarrow$ `[ˈlyː.kə]`) and nasalize vowels before syllable-final `-n` (`Tortôn` `[tɔr.ˈtoːŋ]`, `Meilân` `[mɛɪ.ˈlaːŋ]`).
  2. **Softened Affricates:** Where Standard Tarraconian pronounces initial/post-consonantal `z / tz` strongly as `[ts]`, Alpine-Tarraconian lenites it toward Cisalpine `[s ~ dz]` in rapid speech (*Bisantz* $\rightarrow$ `[bi.ˈzans]`).
  3. **Republican & Municipal Vocabulary:** Retains northern Italian communal terms alongside Imperial German: *Komûn* ("Commune"), *Podestat* ("Podestà / Municipal Magistrate"), *Palatz* ("Civic Palace"), and *Alpe / Mont* ("Alpine Pass").

### 6.2 Croato-Tarraconian (*Kroatisch-Tarraconisch*, `ID 345` — Formed 1057)
* **Linguistic Classification:** Dinaric-Adriatic High German dialect (`language_high_german`) with a **Chakavian/Shtokavian South Slavic** and Dalmatian Romance adstrate.
* **Spoken Zone (1122):** Dalmatia, Slavonia, and Ragusa (`c_cetina`, `c_zagreb`, `c_vukovar`, `c_ragusa`), plus garrisoned imperial fiefs in Ardennes-Lotharingia (`c_luxembourg`, `c_bouillon`, `c_namur`), Languedoc (`c_uses`, `c_montpellier`, `c_carcassonne`, `c_foix`), Jutland (`c_aalborg`), and the Atlas (`c_tadla`, `c_ziz`).
* **Distinctive Phonological & Lexical Features:**
  1. **Slavic Sibilant & Palatal Precision:** Speakers distinguish alveolo-palatal `[tɕ]` (*ć*) from postalveolar `[tʃ]` (*č*, written `tsch`) and syllabic trilled `[r̩]` (as in *Krk* $\rightarrow$ **Vegel / Krk**, *Vrhbosna* $\rightarrow$ **Werchbosne**).
  2. **Vukovar/Pannonian `w` $\rightarrow$ `[v]`:** Initial bilabial `w-` is pronounced strictly as labiodental `[v]` under South Slavic influence (*Wukowâr* `[ˈvu.ko.vaːr]`).
  3. **Dinaric Administrative & Military Lexicon:** Incorporates Croatian terms into High German morphology: *Schûpan* ("Župan / District Lord"), *Bânat* ("Banate / Frontier March"), *Klasûre* ("Defile / Mountain Gorge"), and *Gradina* ("Hillfort").

### 6.3 Ungwârer (*Ungwârerisch / Ungwâri*, `ID 367` — Formed 12 April 1094)
* **Linguistic Classification:** **Germano-Magyar Mixed Daughter Language** (`language_magyar` with `heritage_central_germanic` and `name_list_franconian`).
* **Spoken Zone (1122):** The Northern Hungarian / Carpathian highland corridor (`c_visegrad` *Plintenburch*, `c_hewes` *Êger*, `c_borsod` *Borschot*, `c_spis` *Zips / Tschingouwe*, and `c_abauj` *Ûjwâr*).
* **Historical & Sociolinguistic Genesis:**
  * Named directly from the Tarraconian Chancellery name for *Ungvár* (**`Ungwâr`** `[ˈʊŋ.waːr]` + **`-er`**), **Ungwârer** arose in **1094** when second- and third-generation Tarraconian castle-wardens and border horse-archers in Upper Hungary intermarried with local Magyar-speaking horsemen and Silk Road mercenaries.
  * While retaining their **Central Germanic aristocratic identity**, **Franconian baptismal names** (*Wernhard, Guntram, Adémar, Sieghard, Liutpold*), **Iberian-Tarraconian stone architecture**, and **Imperial HRE heraldry**, the Ungwârer adopted **Old Hungarian (`language_magyar`)** as their daily and military command language.
* **Distinctive Phonological, Grammatical & Lexical Features:**
  1. **Uralic Agglutination on Tarraconian Stems:** Tarraconian administrative and military nouns are inflected with Old Hungarian case suffixes and vowel harmony:
     * *Burch* ("Castle") $\rightarrow$ **Burch-ban** ("in the castle") or compounded with Magyar *vár* as **Burchvár**
     * *Magistrat* $\rightarrow$ **Magisztrát-ok** ("Magistrates")
     * *Marchgrâf* $\rightarrow$ **Markgróf / Határgróf** ("Margrave / Border-Count")
     * *Ungwâr* $\rightarrow$ **Ungwârer** (in Tarraconian) / **Ungváriak** (in spoken Ungwârer Magyar)
  2. **Preservation of Tarraconian Long Circumflex Vowels (`â, ê, î, ô, û`):** Tarraconian long vowels map directly onto Old Hungarian long vowels (*á, é, í, ó, ú*), while the ethnonym itself preserves the Tarraconian Chancellery spelling **`Ungwârer`** (`[ˈʊŋ.waː.rər]`) even in official imperial registers.
  3. **Carpathian-Steppe & Silk Road Military Vocabulary:** Reflecting its *Bellicose* ethos, *Saharan Nomads*, *Winter Warriors*, and 8 Silk Road innovations (*Composite Crossbow*, *Fire Medicine*, *Lacquered Armor*, *Dragon Kiln*), Ungwârer speech blends Franconian knightly terms (*Ritter, Harnisch, Banner*) with Magyar and Steppe equestrian terminology (*Hadnagy* "lieutenant", *Végvár* "border fortress", *Tábor* "encampment", and *Tüzes-nyíl* "fire-arrow").
